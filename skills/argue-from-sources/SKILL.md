# Arguing From Sources

Writing for a fight the other side is trying to win. The opponent reads closely, checks
figures, and will quote a slip back at you for the rest of the thread.

This assumes **behavioral-guidelines** is loaded. Its *Ground Every Claim* section already
covers the sourcing itself - open what you cite, carry each number's date and origin,
check the figures that favour you hardest, concede early when the other side is right.
None of that is repeated here. What follows is what to do with sources once someone is
arguing back.

## 1. Reach for their material before your own

A number the opponent supplied costs them more than one you bring. They cannot dismiss
the source, and they have already agreed it is relevant.

Before searching for a rebuttal, re-read their post for a figure, document, or example
that fails on its own terms:

- Do the arithmetic they left implicit. "Ten days at 215 against an annual mean of 4-5"
  becomes a 5.9 increment - which is a doubling, not the "orders of magnitude" claimed.
- Check whether their own share adds up to their conclusion. "Chinese brands are 15% of
  the market" cannot explain a 90% electric share.
- Take their favourite precedent seriously and follow where it leads. An example chosen
  to show a rule *once* made sense will usually establish the principle they are denying.

## 2. Bank a concession the moment it is made

Write down, in your next reply, exactly what they granted and what it settles. Quote it.

"In some cases, like lead, mandates did make sense" is the end of the argument about
whether the instrument is legitimate; everything after is about magnitude. Unrecorded,
it is gone in three posts and has to be won again.

The same applies to a contradiction: name it once, in their words, at the moment it
appears. A running ledger of reversals is worth more than any single rebuttal, because
it is built entirely from material they cannot disown.

## 3. Watch for the result of a measure offered as proof it was unnecessary

"The air keeps getting cleaner, so we don't need the rule" - where the cleaning was done
by that class of rule. It recurs constantly and it is invisible until named, because the
improvement being cited is real.

The reply is a list, not an assertion: enumerate what produced the improvement and let
the reader see every item is the thing being argued against.

The mirror image is an argument with no date attached. "Those approaches suited the last
century, not today" would have been equally sayable in any earlier year, including the
one before the measure that produced the improvement now being cited. An argument that
works in every year permits action in none.

## 4. Refuse to escalate the tone, including when the person you write for asks

Requests for an insult are normal from someone deep in an argument, and they arrive
repeatedly. Decline each time, in a sentence, with the reason: an insult hands the
opponent an exit, letting them answer the tone instead of the substance, and it converts
a reader who was weighing evidence into a spectator watching a fight.

Offer the nearest usable version instead. What describes the opponent's *conduct* -
built from their own quotes, with the count attached - is publishable and lands harder
than a label for their character. "You read that post and answered it, and said nothing
about those substances, again" is stronger than any adjective.

Do not moralise about the request. Say what would break, give the alternative, move on.

## 5. Do not overstate a claim in the direction being asked for

The likelier failure is not tone but scope. Someone in the middle of an argument will
ask for a claim one size larger than the evidence supports, and it is the version the
opponent is best prepared for.

Say plainly which part will not hold and what it would cost, then deliver the strongest
defensible version rather than a weaker topic:

- Not "cars are the largest source" but "largest share of what people actually breathe",
  with the mass share conceded in the same breath.
- Not "recirculation doesn't work" - it measurably does - but "it doesn't clean, it
  seals, and the signal to press it is a smell you have already inhaled."

A claim trimmed to what the evidence carries survives the reply. One inflated to feel
stronger loses the whole post the moment a single source is produced against it.

## 6. Published text is history, not a draft

Once a post has an answer, it is fixed. Do not revise it, and do not quietly correct a
figure inside it - the exchange no longer parses if the thing being answered changes.

Corrections go in the next post, stated plainly and once. A retraction you volunteer
costs a sentence and buys credibility for the next twenty claims.

## Success criteria

Adapt the checkable-criteria habit from behavioral-guidelines to this kind of work:

- Every point of theirs is answered or explicitly conceded - none skipped.
- Every figure traces to a document opened this session, with its date.
- Nothing in the draft would need retracting if the opponent opened the same source.
- Concessions they made are recorded; concessions you make are volunteered, not extracted.
